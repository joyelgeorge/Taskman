# Lane: free fixes, for the credential rather than the fee

**Small, free, and deliberately unpaid — because the thing being bought is
proof, and this project has never had any.**

## The gap it fills

The agency offer's central claim is *"a re-run after the fixes, showing the
findings gone."* `proveFixed()` now exists to produce that. But **no fix has
ever been delivered to anyone**, so the claim has no instance behind it. Selling
a deliverable that has never been delivered once is a weaker position than
giving three away.

## The lane

Pick **three** targets from the seven verified findings. Offer the fix free, in
full, with no conditions. Deliver:

1. The finding, re-derived from a fresh clone.
2. The patch.
3. **The re-run showing it gone** — `proveFixed()` output, timestamped.

Then, with the owner's written permission, publish the before/after as an
anonymised case.

## What it buys, honestly

- **A worked instance of the deliverable**, which the agency pitch currently
  asserts without evidence.
- **Three named humans** who have received real value — level 2 on the board,
  and the warmest possible source of a referral or a testimonial.
- **Material for the teardown series** that is not self-referential: every
  published piece so far is about our own scanner. A fix that landed on someone
  else's product is a different kind of evidence.

## What it does not buy

Money. It is explicitly unpaid, it is not a trial close, and it must not be
pitched as one — `CLAUDE.md` binds the free pass as unconditional, and a free
fix with a follow-up invoice attached is the pattern that gets people reported.

## Done looks like

One free fix delivered with its proof, and the owner's answer — yes, no, or
silence — recorded through `scripts/outreach.mjs`.

## Why three and not thirty

Three is enough to know whether anyone accepts a free fix from a stranger, which
is the actual open question. Thirty is a job.
